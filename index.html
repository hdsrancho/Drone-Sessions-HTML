What even is a drone

An aircraft with no pilot on board, flown remotely or autonomously. Also called a UAV, an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle.

Airframe

The body, arms and wings that carry everything.

Propulsion

Motors and propellers that create thrust.

Brain

A flight controller and sensors that keep it stable.

Applications of drones

Photography and film

Aerial shots without a helicopter.

Agriculture

Crop health and precise spraying.

Delivery

Medicine and parcels to hard places.

Search and rescue

Fast views of disaster zones.

Inspection and mapping

Bridges, power lines and terrain.

Defense and security

Surveillance and patrol.

Types of aircraft

Rotor-based

Spinning blades lift and steer. Hovers and lands anywhere. Example: quadcopter.

Fixed-wing

Wings make lift as it moves forward. Efficient and fast, but needs a launch and cannot hover.

Hybrid

Rotors for vertical takeoff, wings for efficient cruise. Best of both, with more complexity.

Laws of physics

Lift

Pushes up, against weight.

Weight

Gravity pulls down.

Thrust

Pushes forward.

Drag

Resists motion.

Newton’s third law: a propeller pushes air down, so the air pushes the drone up. Steady flight means all four forces balance.

Airfoil

Chord line
Straight line from leading edge to trailing edge.

Camber line
Curve halfway between the upper and lower surface.

Angle of attack (α)
Angle between the chord line and the airflow.

Lift
Force perpendicular to the airflow.

Aerodynamics

Lift

L = ½ ρ v² S CL

Drag

D = ½ ρ v² S CD

Lift and drag grow with the square of speed and with wing area. Past a critical angle of attack the airflow separates from the wing and lift collapses. This is a stall.
